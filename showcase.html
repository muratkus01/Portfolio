Linear Program Formulation & Storage Dynamics

The prosumer energy management system schedules residential flexible storage to minimize total net electricity cost over the H quarter-hour steps (Δt = 0.25 h) of a rolling window that ends at the last published day-ahead price (11 to 35 h; day D+1 prices are known from 13:00 on day D), re-solved every 15 minutes:

min Σt=1H [ λtimp · Ptgrid,imp - λtexp · Ptgrid,exp + cdeg · Ptdis ] Δt - vT · SoCH · Enom

Export remuneration in the published results is wholesale-indexed (λtexp = max(0, (1 + VAT) · λtspot - 0.01 EUR/kWh)), with no EEG feed-in tariff; import costs λtimp = (1 + VAT) · λtspot + 0.1937 EUR/kWh (the thesis tariff). The sizing simulator below still uses a fixed 7.86 ct/kWh feed-in tariff. Non-ideal storage dynamics enforce a round-trip efficiency of 0.894 (ηch = ηdis = 0.945) with wear cdeg = 0.0544 EUR per kWh discharged. vT values the energy left at the window end (SoCH · Enom, with Enom = 9.37 kWh) at a monthly price learned on 2024; B2 runs to the end of the test period and has none:

SoCt+1 = SoCt + [ ηch · Ptch - Ptdis / ηdis ] · (Δt / Enom) 0.05 ≤ SoCt ≤ 0.95, 0 ≤ Ptch, 0 ≤ Ptdis, Ptch + Ptdis ≤ Pinv

Inverter conversion bounds enforce instantaneous power balance: Ptgrid,imp - Ptgrid,exp + Ptpv + Ptdis - Ptch = Ptload.

German Regulatory Framework: §14a & §41a EnWG

Under statutory German energy law (§41a EnWG), large electricity suppliers must offer dynamic spot retail tariffs linked directly to the EPEX SPOT Day-Ahead hourly/quarter-hourly clearing price. This exposes residential consumers to wholesale price volatility, creating economic incentives for automated price arbitrage.

Under §14a EnWG (effective January 2024), Distribution System Operators (DSOs) possess the legal right to dynamically throttle controllable consumer devices (Steuerbare Verbrauchseinrichtungen - SteuVE) including home batteries and heat pumps down to a 4.2 kW safety ceiling during grid congestion, while granting fixed or volumetric network charge reductions (Module 1-3).

No published P01 result applies §14a: the tariff used has no network-charge module and no dimming.

Production Invariants & Edge Cases Caught

1. End-of-Horizon Hoarding Defect: An early receding-horizon formulation valued the energy left in the battery at the horizon boundary (t=96) at the retail import price. The solver then imported grid power right before every boundary to "hoard" value it could never realise, and on a PV-rich summer week the MPC lost to the price-blind rule. The fix values a stored kWh by what it will replace: first a blend of import and export prices, weighted by the share of the window in which the site imports; the deployable B3 now uses a monthly value learned on 2024 from the perfect-foresight LP's shadow prices.

2. RL Action Clipping Was Not the Main Defect: The safety layer clipped 77 to 82% of the first Soft Actor-Critic (SAC) policy's actions: the requested power (an action in [-1, 1] times the inverter rating) often lay outside what the state of charge and the limits allowed. Mapping each action onto the feasible range removes the clipping (0%), but on its own it changes nothing reliable: a median of -3.4% of the B1-to-B2 gap, against -7.3% for the first policy. The differential reward, which pays the agent only for the cost difference its battery makes, is what fixes it: +27.0% alone and +32.4% with both changes (medians of 3 seeds; reports/rl_ablation_* and reports/rl_eval_H28).

Benchmarking Progression & Solve Time

The project benchmarks four controller rungs to quantify exact value capture:

• B1 Self-Consumption Rule: Greedy heuristic that charges the battery whenever rooftop PV exceeds home demand, and discharges whenever load exceeds PV (Baseline 0% headroom recovery).

• B2 Perfect Foresight LP: One LP over the whole 625-day test period with exact knowledge of load, PV and prices, solved by HiGHS (100% ceiling).

• B3 Rolling MPC: Re-solved every 15 minutes over a window that ends at the last published day-ahead price (11 to 35 h), using the published prices, a day-ahead NWP PV forecast and the BDEW H0 load profile (captures 89.4% of the B1-to-B2 headroom for household H28).

• Learned RL (Rescaled SAC): Soft Actor-Critic trained for 500,000 steps on 2024 with the same information as B3, rescaled actions and a differential reward (recovers 32.4% of the headroom, median of 3 seeds).

Solve time: the deployable B3 needs 19.5 ms per window on average in the committed run (reports/rolling_eval_htw_H28/rolling_eval_summary.csv, column mean_solve_ms); an earlier run recorded 5.9 ms (the same file at commit 82e2d30). Either way, each window solves far inside its 15-minute step.

Master Thesis Sensitivity Methodology (Table 3-4 & Chapter 8)

The thesis implements two systematic sensitivity analyses to isolate asset sizing boundaries:

• Type 1: 3D Grid Reduction (1,016 Feasible Configs): The full parameter space of PV (2-15 kWp), BESS (2-15 kWh), and Inverter (2-15 kW) spans 2,744 combinations. Three physical coupling criteria (PVS ratio ∈ [0.5, 2.0], EPR ∈ [0.5, 2.0], and Inverter Loading IL ∈ [0.5, 2.0]) eliminate unphysical oversizing, pruning the domain to exactly 1,016 feasible configurations.

• Balance-of-System (BoS) CAPEX Model (Table 3-2): CAPEX = 1,400 · Ppv + 650 · Ebess + 300 · Pinv + 1,200 EUR.

• Top Economic Configuration: Optimal balance achieved at 15 kWp PV / 8 kWh BESS / 8 kW Inverter with CAPEX 29,800 EUR, Net Cost -1,475.98 EUR/yr, Net Savings 2,108.98 EUR/yr, ROCE 7.08%, Self-Consumption 45.4%, and Self-Sufficiency 78.4%.

• Type 2: Targeted Sensitivity Analysis: Evaluates 2D BESS-Inverter matrices for fixed PV capacities (5, 10, 15 kWp). Confirms optimal battery capacity sweet spots (4-5 kWh for 5 kWp PV, 6-8 kWh for 10 kWp PV, and 8-10 kWh for 15 kWp PV). BESS expansion beyond 10 kWh encounters diminishing returns where additional storage CAPEX exceeds incremental summer spot arbitrage gains.

Project 02 stops at B3, on purpose

A learned policy is worth training where a rolling MPC cannot keep up with the problem, and this model does not pose such a problem yet. Each reason can be checked in the code and the tables:

1. No rung commits a day-ahead schedule. market.settle prices a deviation from a committed schedule at reBAP, but no rung produces one and no command passes one, so imbalance_cost_eur is 0 in every row of every table.

2. No redispatch. market.redispatch_series exists, but no command calls it, and tables.INERT_SETTINGS names its settings as unused.

3. The connection does not bind on national profiles. At 60 MW, steps_over_connection is 0 in every Energy-Charts window of reports/window_stats.csv: the plant's peak generation is 42.5 MW in 2024 and 38.3 MW in 2025.

4. The premium is deterministic. It is a fixed function of the period's prices and national generation. B3 plans on an estimate of it, the previous month's realised rate; in July 2025 that estimate, 27.34 EUR/MWh, lies far above the 8.74 EUR/MWh B3 is settled on. Narrowing that gap needs a better estimate of the market value, a forecasting task, not a learned policy.

Reinforcement learning is shown in Project 01, on a household PV battery: a soft actor-critic agent reaches 32.4% of the gain from B1 to B2 (median of three seeds), against 89.4% for its rolling MPC. Here it is future work, worth doing once every rung commits a day-ahead schedule that is settled at reBAP.

Linear Program, Plant Model and Settlement

B2 and B3 solve one linear program with no binary variables: with wear priced, charging and discharging at once never pays. It maximises revenue over the quarter-hours (Δt = 0.25 h) of the window, or of B3's horizon, at an effective price that adds the market premium only where it is payable:

max Σt [ pt · et - cw · (cht + dist) ] Δt, pt = λtDA + MPt · 1[premium payable in t] - fee

subject to, in every quarter-hour t:

gt ≤ Gtwind + Gtpv, et = gt + dist - cht, 0 ≤ et ≤ 60 MW, cht ≤ gt (no grid charging)
soct = soct-1 + ( ηc · cht - dist / ηd ) Δt, socmin ≤ soct ≤ socmax, 0 ≤ cht, dist ≤ 20 MW

G is the wind and PV available, g what the plant uses of it; the rest is curtailed, and curtailment is split by cause: forced by the connection, or chosen. Wind and PV are the national capacity factor of each quarter-hour, Energy-Charts generation over the fleet of its month, times the rating, with no power curve, wake model or inverter clipping. The battery runs between 5% and 95% of its 40 MWh at 95% efficiency each way, starts every window at its minimum and wears at cw = 3 EUR per MWh charged or discharged. No value is put on the energy left at the end of a plan (see Invariant Guards). The settlement pays the day-ahead price on the export and the premium where it is payable, and charges the 3 EUR/MWh marketing fee and the wear.

EEG Market Premium and the Negative-Price Rule by Vintage

Under EEG direct marketing the law pays each technology max(0, applicable value - market value) on its own export, wherever the premium is payable: against the monthly market value for plants commissioned or awarded before 2023, against the annual value from 2023 on (EEG 2023 Anlage 1 Nr. 2). The model pays the plant one blended rate per period, the two premiums weighted by its own available generation. The market values are computed proxies, the day-ahead price weighted by national feed-in; the values the TSOs publish, which the premium is legally settled on, are not loaded. The default plant takes the Bundesnetzagentur's ceilings for the 2025 tenders, 73.5 EUR/MWh for wind and 68.0 for PV, not an award, and is paid against the annual value.

EEG §51, as implemented by vintage: no rule before 2016; six hours (2016-2020) or four hours (2021-2022) of negative prices remove the premium for the whole run; for the vintage from 2023 to 24 February 2025 the threshold falls by calendar year, three hours in 2024 and 2025; from 25 February 2025 (Solarspitzengesetz) the premium goes in every negative period. Runs are counted in calendar hours, and an hour counts as negative when the mean of its quarter-hour prices is negative. Not modelled: the §51a extension of the support period and the small-plant exemptions.

Result: every tightening of the rule lowers B0's revenue and raises the energy B2 chooses to curtail, for both vintages in both years. Under the rule of the newest plants, a counterfactual before 25 February 2025, B2 curtails 6,715 MWh in 2024 and 8,155 MWh in 2025 at the default plant. Paid per technology, as the law pays, B0 loses 144,002 EUR of premium to that rule in 2024 and 128,716 EUR in 2025; the blended rate, which every table settles on, takes 116,440 and 62,246 EUR, so it understates what a rule removes. Sources: reports/negprice_2024.csv and reports/negprice_2025.csv.

Not modelled: intraday trading, a day-ahead schedule settled at reBAP, and Redispatch 2.0. aFRR participation by the battery is not built yet: its settings in MarketConfig are inert.

The Ladder Invariant and the Defects It Caught

No rung may beat B2, perfect foresight, on revenue before imbalance, beyond the solver's rounding (ladder_tolerance_eur in every table); baselines.run_ladder raises otherwise. No row of any table breaks a plant limit, settles any imbalance or buys grid energy.

1. Market value from the plant's own export: a controller that correctly curtails during negative prices concentrates its output in high-price hours. That raised its own apparent market value and cut its own premium, so perfect foresight scored below a do-nothing baseline. The market value is a national figure that one plant cannot move, so it is now exogenous, and the invariant now covers every rung, not only B3.

2. Plan execution: B3 applied the curtailment fraction it planned on forecast generation to the true generation, and so discarded energy the connection could have taken. It now executes planned curtailment only where the effective price is negative; elsewhere the safety layer stores what the connection cannot take, as far as the battery has room, and curtails the rest.

3. Terminal value: a linear value on the energy stored at the end of each plan made B3 hoard energy. On the showcase's three synthetic days (the default plant behind a 40 MW connection, a 12-hour horizon, a fixed premium of 8 EUR/MWh), B3 recovers 100.0% of the B1-to-B2 gain with perfect forecasts and 87.1% with a 15% forecast error, but -142.4% and -179.1% with the terminal value on, ending with 34.3 and 38.0 MWh stored that it never sells. So the terminal value is off by default. These figures come from synthetic inputs, not from data: reports/b3_ablation_synthetic_demo.csv.

Controller Ladder, B0 to B3

• B0 No Battery: the same generation without a battery. It curtails only what the connection forces, and sells through negative prices.

• B1 Rule-Based Storage: stores what the connection would spill and discharges into headroom. It reads no prices, so it equals B0 wherever the connection never binds.

• B2 Perfect-Foresight LP: one LP over the whole window on the realised premium, the ceiling of the ladder. "B2 no battery" is B2 with the battery taken out, perfect foresight that can only curtail; it splits B2's gain over B0 into the battery's own value and the value of curtailing at low prices.

• B3 Rolling LP-MPC: re-solves the LP every quarter-hour over the next 24 hours and applies the first step. Its forecasts are synthetic: wind and PV are the truth times (1 + 0.15 · AR(1)) with a step-to-step correlation of 0.8, and the price forecast adds 15% of the 2024 day-ahead price's standard deviation times such an error. One error series serves every issue time, so the error does not grow with lead time; a forecast layer is not built. B3 plans each month on the previous month's realised premium and puts no value on the energy left at the end of a plan. It takes 0.23 s per solve on average in both 2025 windows (b3_solve_time_mean_s); four runs shared a 4-core laptop, so that is an order of magnitude only.

• RL: not implemented; see "Project 02 stops at B3" above.

03 Probabilistic Forecast-to-Bid for a German Wind and PV Portfolio

A balance responsible party sells the output of a 200 MW wind + 100 MW PV portfolio, shaped like the German fleet, in the day-ahead auction, may correct the position intraday as forecasts improve, and settles what is left at the German imbalance price, the reBAP. A ladder of quantile forecasts, from climatology to LightGBM on archived weather forecasts from earlier model runs, is scored against the forecast the TSOs publish: in pinball loss, and in euros at real day-ahead, intraday (ID AEP) and reBAP prices.

Pinball Loss, Settlement, and Why Euros Do Not Simply Follow Skill

Every rung predicts nine quantiles (τ = 0.1, 0.2, ..., 0.9) of the portfolio's output at every decision stage, scored by the pinball loss:

Lτ(y, qτ) = max( τ · (y - qτ), (1 - τ) · (qτ - y) )

The day-ahead position earns the day-ahead price. Each intraday trade ΔQs earns the ID AEP and pays a half-spread cs (3 EUR/MWh, doubling toward the last stage) plus 0.02 EUR/MWh per MW traded. What is left settles at reBAP, one price for short and long:

R = Σt [ λtDA · QtDA + Σs ( λtID · ΔQt,s - (cs + 0.02 · |ΔQt,s|) · |ΔQt,s| ) + reBAPt · (Yt - Qtfinal) ] · Δt

For a day-ahead schedule f, the result against the perfect forecast is exact, with error e = y - f, spread s = reBAP - DA price and H the hours evaluated:

R(f) - R(y) = Σt et · st · Δt = H · mean(e) · mean(s) + H · cov(e, s)

The first term, the bias part, pays a schedule below the output, which leaves the portfolio long, while reBAP averages above the day-ahead price (by 4.42 EUR/MWh in the test window). The second, the alignment part, charges every error that runs with the system's imbalance. This portfolio is the German fleet in miniature, so a good forecast's remaining errors are the system's own errors: in both windows the TSO forecast's error correlates more strongly with the spread than any other rung's. Sources: reports/test/error_alignment.csv and reports/validation/error_alignment.csv.

German Day-Ahead, Intraday and reBAP Settlement

Every quarter-hour is decided at the day-ahead gate closure, 12:00 Europe/Berlin on the day before delivery (a lead of 12 to 35.75 hours, 36.75 on the 25-hour day when the clocks go back), and again 12, 6, 2 and 0.5 hours before delivery, never before the intraday market opens at 16:00 the day before. Day-ahead products are hourly until 2025-09-30 and quarter-hourly after.

Intraday trades clear at the ID AEP: the volume-weighted price of the last 500 MW traded in continuous intraday for each quarter-hour, published by the four German TSOs on netztransparenz.de. A trade decided twelve hours ahead would have met a different price, and the half-spread is an assumption, swept from 0 to 6 EUR/MWh. What is left settles at the quality-assured reBAP, which had one price for short and long positions in every quarter-hour downloaded. With one price for both directions the newsvendor critical-fractile argument has no basis, so the NV policy is kept only as an ablation.

The TSO forecasts carry no publication time. The EU transparency rules make the day-ahead forecast due by 18:00 on the day before delivery, after the 12:00 gate closure, so F4 may hold information the other rungs could not have had: it is a benchmark, not a competitor. The portfolio is a price taker.

No Look-Ahead: Checks That Can Fail

Information sets: a forecast for delivery step t decided at step i sees measured output up to one hour before i, the newest weather run already published at i (a run is taken as public six hours after it was made, an assumption), and the calendar and sun position of t. A test corrupts everything outside that set and requires identical features.

Look-ahead audit: it counts forecasts read after their decision time, and a test shows that it catches a cheating stage. The old audit only re-checked the forecast store's own guard, so it could not fail.

Errors found and fixed in the rebuild: day-ahead positions read forecasts issued after the 12:00 gate closure for every quarter-hour delivered after noon (a fixed 24-hour lead); a PV seasonal-persistence feature wrapped around to the end of the series on the first day of data, so it read the future (the information-set test caught it); the forecast store answered a lead beyond its horizon with another quarter-hour's forecast; and the reported "CRPS" was twice the mean pinball loss over the levels 0.1 to 0.9, so the scores now report the pinball loss under its own name. Each fix ships with a regression test that fails on the old code.

Withdrawn: an earlier version of this page showed a large intraday trading gain of a "stochastic deadband MPC" over 30 days. It came from a model in which the intraday price equalled the day-ahead price, reBAP was synthetic and the forecasts were the realised output plus hand-set noise, and it is withdrawn.

Forecast Ladder and Trading Policies

• F0 Climatology: quantiles of the output by calendar month and local hour.

• F1 Persistence: wind as last measured, PV as on the last measured day, plus residual quantiles by hour.

• F2 Linear Quantile Regression: on 16 aggregate features.

• F3 LightGBM: one model per quantile level on all features, three seeds; it stops early and recalibrates on the last 60 days of the training window, held out.

• F4 TSO Forecast: the published forecast plus residual quantiles by hour, as a benchmark.

Training from 2024-02-18, validation 2025-07-02 to 2025-12-29 (it tunes only B3's band), test 2026-01-01 to 2026-10-14, with gaps of at least 36 hours between the windows.

Policies: B0 schedules the day-ahead median and never corrects. B1 trades to the newest median at every intraday stage. B3 trades only the part of a revision beyond k times the stage's q90 - q10 band (k = 4 for F1 to F3 and 1 for F4, from the validation window):

ΔQs = sign(δs) · max( |δs| - k · (q90s - q10s), 0 ), δs = q50s - Qpos

NV schedules one quantile, 0.533, chosen from the imbalance-cost asymmetry of the training data (an ablation). REF schedules the realised output: the perfect forecast.

Intraday result: following every revision (B1) loses money against scheduling once with every forecast that revises, in both windows, at every half-spread from 0.5 EUR/MWh; with the TSO forecast it loses 87,261 EUR in 2026 even at zero cost. A likely reason: the revisions are public, and every trade here clears at the ID AEP, an index of the last trades before delivery, so the price already carries them. B3's band, tuned on the validation window, does not transfer: with the TSO forecast it loses 69,112 EUR against B0 in 2026 at a half-spread of 3 EUR/MWh. Sources: reports/test/liquidity.csv and policies.csv, and reports/validation/liquidity.csv.
